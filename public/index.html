How these colours are decided (test formula)
A computer adds up two simple checks: how much rain fell in the hills upstream in the last two days, and how the nearest river compares with the same time of year over the past ten years. Lots of rain or a high river turns a village yellow; both together turn it red. For a few basins a tested river model gives the colour instead (the popup says which). The cut-off numbers are trial values picked by the builders - they have not been checked against real floods, which is why this map is only a test.

How to read this map

This map shows three kinds of information. They are not the same thing:

1. Official alerts (coloured strip at the top, and the first box in the side panel): the government's own warnings, word for word. Always follow these first. Each shows when it was issued - a river can crest and start falling while its alert is still active.

2. River points (round icons on the rivers): the official gauge at that spot. Blue = below the official warning mark, yellow = above warning, red = above danger. The arrow shows whether the measured water is rising, steady or falling right now.

3. Village dots: this map's own estimate for the day ahead - green (no unusual signs), yellow (worth watching), red (strong signs - estimate only). A dashed ring means the village position is approximate. Tap any dot: a tag says whether the colour came from a tested river model ("our forecast") or a simple trial formula ("our estimate") - and these can be wrong. Each village takes its colour from the nearest covered river, so villages on the same river share the same outlook - the colour is about the river's behaviour, not about that one village.

Now or later? Alerts speak about now and the next hours (as issued). Gauge readings and their arrows are the latest measurement. Village colours are our estimate for the day ahead. Lines like "CWC expects…" are the official outlook for the coming days.

Whose word is it? Alerts, gauge readings and the warning/danger marks are the Government of India's (CWC, ASDMA, IMD). The river-flow history behind our estimates comes from the EU's official global flood system (Copernicus) and its radar satellites. The models that combine them are this project's own - built with standard methods and tested openly, but approved by no authority. That is why their colours are estimates.

Data sources. Official river gauges, warning/danger marks and alerts: CWC, ASDMA and IMD (via India-WRIS and NDMA SACHET). River-flow history and forecasts: the EU Copernicus global flood system (GloFAS, relayed by Open-Meteo); rainfall from Open-Meteo. Satellite flood extent: Copernicus Sentinel-1. Dam reservoir levels: NERLDC. Villages and basemap: OpenStreetMap. NASA GPM IMERG and IMD gridded rainfall are used to cross-check the rain, not to colour the map. Every figure in a popup names its own source.

This is a test, not a warning service. Real flood warnings come from ASDMA, CWC and your District Administration - never from this map.
